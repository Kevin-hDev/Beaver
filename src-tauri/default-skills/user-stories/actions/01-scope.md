# 01 - Scope

You establish the user outcome and decide whether the input is one story or an epic.

## Input

- Accept a product outcome, epic, feature, PRD, or accepted requirement set.

## Output

- Return the user, outcomes, boundaries, constraints, assumptions, and classification.

## Process

1. **Recover outcome.** You identify who needs what outcome and why it matters.
2. **Set boundaries.** You capture in-scope and excluded outcomes without choosing implementation.
3. **Find decisions.** You separate accepted requirements, assumptions, open questions, and dependencies.
4. **Classify breadth.** You use one story for one independently valuable outcome and an epic for several outcomes.
5. **Ask minimally.** You ask at most three related questions per round when user, outcome, boundary, or acceptance meaning is missing. You continue focused rounds until every blocking scope question is resolved or explicitly deferred.
6. **Confirm.** You show the scope and epic-or-story classification and wait for explicit user approval before slicing.

## Stop conditions

- You stop when the product problem or user outcome is not defined enough to create valuable stories.
- You do not translate technical layers directly into stories.

## Test

- The scope states user, outcome, boundaries, constraints, and epic-or-story classification.
- No blocking product question remains hidden.
- The user approves the scope and classification before slicing starts.
