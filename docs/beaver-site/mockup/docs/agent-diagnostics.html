Diagnostics and errors

Something failed: this page explains how to read the error, how to know whether retrying is safe, and what Beaver keeps — or does not — in its records.

How to read an error

Every tool failure carries three pieces of information: a code, the precise identifier of the cause; a category, the nature of the problem; and a retry flag, which says whether retrying is safe.

The third is the one that helps you most: it saves you from rerunning an action that will fail identically, or worse, one that had already partly succeeded.

Can you retry?

The retry flag is true only when a new attempt is safe without checking external state. The nuance matters: a timeout on a write cannot be replayed blindly, because the write may have gone through; a read that fails, on the other hand, can be replayed at no risk.

Not every error can be replayed A write operation interrupted by a timeout may have gone through. Beaver states explicitly when a new attempt is safe.

The diagnostics

Beaver keeps diagnostic records per conversation: what was attempted, with which summarized arguments, and what failed. They serve to understand after the fact a conversation that went wrong, without rereading the whole history. They are not carried over into a clone.

What is masked in the diagnostics

The recorded arguments are summarized and redacted before writing:

  • text is truncated at 200 characters;
  • full structures at 1,000 characters;
  • sensitive values are replaced by [redacted];
  • paths are replaced by [path].
Your data is not in the diagnostics A record makes it possible to understand what happened without keeping the content of your files or your secrets.
