Your subscription, without a key

You already have a subscription with a provider and you would rather not open a developer account or manage separate billing. Three providers accept an account sign-in: you authenticate with them, Beaver receives a token, and that is all.

What an account sign-in actually grants does not depend on Beaver but on the provider: depending on the subscription, the available models and the usage limits differ. An account sign-in is not necessarily equivalent to an API key.

Warning The Kimi sign-in is experimental. It may be unstable or change. An API key remains the reliable option for that provider.

Signing in to OpenAI

  • Beaver opens your browser on OpenAI's sign-in page.
  • You authenticate with OpenAI — never inside Beaver.
  • OpenAI redirects back to Beaver, which receives the reply on a small local server, active only during the sign-in.
  • Beaver exchanges that reply for access tokens.

Two protections are worth knowing about. Beaver never sees your password: authentication happens entirely on the provider's site. And the request is tied to the session by a single-use secret generated at the start — a third party intercepting the reply could do nothing with it without that secret.

Once connected, Beaver shows the account's address, extracted from the token received. It serves only to tell which account is linked.

Signing in to xAI or Kimi

These two providers use a different mechanism, designed for devices without a built-in browser:

  • Beaver shows a short code and a web address.
  • You open that address, sign in, and enter the code.
  • Meanwhile, Beaver polls the provider at regular intervals until authorization is granted.

Progress is shown live in Beaver: starting, waiting, success, cancellation, failure. The sign-in is abandoned after 15 minutes. You can cancel at any time; cancellation is immediate and clean.

Where the tokens go

Into the same encrypted vault as the API keys, with the same protections: encryption on disk, master key in the system password manager, no read command exposed to the interface, memory erased after use. The details are in The vault and API keys.

Each provider has its own separate entry in the vault: signing out of one does not touch the other.

Tokens are validated before being saved — length capped at 4,096 characters, consistent expiry date. An aberrant token is refused rather than stored.

Security Beaver never sees your password. Authentication takes place entirely on the provider's site; Beaver only receives an access token, which can be revoked.

Automatic renewal

An access token has a limited lifetime. Beaver renews it on its own, one minute before its actual expiry — that margin prevents a request from leaving with a token that expires en route.

You have nothing to do. An established connection stays valid as long as you do not sign out and the provider does not revoke access.

A generation mechanism protects against a subtle case: if you sign in again while a renewal is in flight, the stale renewal does not overwrite the new connection. Without that protection, a fresh sign-in could be cancelled seconds later by an operation started before it.

Signing out

Signing out cancels any sign-in in progress and erases the tokens from the vault. The provider goes back to the disconnected state and its models leave the selector.

Warning Signing out of Beaver does not revoke access at the provider. To cut access off entirely, also go through the security page of your account with them.

What can fail

FailureWhat it means

The messages shown stay deliberately generic — "Unable to connect", "Sign-in cancelled". The technical detail goes to the logs, not to the screen.
