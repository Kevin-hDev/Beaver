How much the model should think

Some models produce an internal train of thought before answering. That work improves hard answers, costs time and tokens on easy questions, and is not configured the same way from one model to the next. This page explains the effort setting and what it implies.

What reasoning is

Some models think before answering: they produce an internal train of thought — hypotheses, checks, backtracking — then write their answer from that work.

That train of thought is billed like the rest and takes up room in the context, even though it does not appear in the final answer. Hence the point of a setting: on a simple question, thinking at length costs without adding anything.

The effort setting

Beaver exposes a common scale, from the lightest to the most thorough.

The eight steps of the effort scale
SettingWhat it asks for
OffAnswer with no thinking step
AutomaticLet the model decide
LowBrief thinking
MediumBalanced thinking
HighThorough thinking
Very highExtended thinking
MaxAs far as the model will go
UltraAn extra step, on the rare models that offer it

Why the options change from model to model

Every provider exposes its own setting, with its own steps. Beaver only shows those the selected model actually accepts. That is not an inconsistency in the interface: offering a step the model ignores would amount to showing a setting with no effect.

  • Some models have no setting at all — they do not reason, or they always reason the same way.
  • Some cannot be turned off — their thinking is part of how they work.
  • The number of steps ranges from two to six depending on the model.

One rule holds everywhere: an unsupported setting is never forced through. Rather than risk a refusal from the provider, Beaver falls back to the closest supported step, in this order: Medium, then Automatic, then the first available step other than Off. So switching model mid-conversation never leaves an orphaned setting.

Note The options change from model to model, and that is normal. A missing setting means that model does not offer it — Beaver only shows the steps actually accepted.

What it costs

  • Time. High effort can multiply several times over the delay before the first line of an answer.
  • Money, on a billed model: reasoning consumes tokens, often at the output rate.
  • Context. The train of thought takes up room, so it reduces what is left for the conversation by as much.

In practice: keep effort low or automatic by default, and raise it for the tasks that really warrant it — a hard bug, a design decision, an analysis. On a rewording or a factual question, high effort costs without adding anything.

Which effort for which task
TaskRecommended effort
Rewording, translating, summarizingOff or Low
Writing simple code, fixing an obvious mistakeLow
Ordinary conversationAutomatic
Debugging a problem that does not reproduceHigh
Designing an architecture, choosing between optionsHigh to Max
Analyzing an entire systemMax
Warning Reasoning is billed and takes up context. It does not appear in the answer but it consumes tokens: high effort on every message shows up in your consumption. See Usage and costs.

Seeing the reasoning

When a model exposes its train of thought, Beaver shows it separately from the answer, in a collapsible area.

  • Some providers deliver a summary of the reasoning rather than its full text. That is their choice, not a truncation by Beaver.
  • Some local models wrap their thinking in tags inside the text. Beaver spots and extracts them so the reasoning does not appear mixed in with the answer.

Special cases

  • Local models — most only offer "off" or "automatic". A few recent families accept three steps.
  • Models with mandatory thinking — the setting is absent or limited to "automatic".
  • Google — the translation depends on the model generation. On Gemini 2.5 generation models, effort becomes a numeric thinking budget, in tokens: 1,024 for low effort, 8,192 by default, 24,576 for high effort. On more recent generations, a named thinking level is sent instead of a number. In both cases, turning reasoning off is indeed sent as a deactivation.
  • Some recent models add steps beyond the usual maximum.
